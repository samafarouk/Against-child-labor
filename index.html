01 / Youssef's story

A day that begins too early.

Read slowly. Imagine the sounds, the rooms, and the responsibilities. This is a case-based story built from the details shared by the debate team.

Youssef is an anonymised case scenario. The accompanying images are illustrative visualisations, not documentary photographs of the child or his family.
06:30 AM · THE WORKSHOP

Before the day has properly begun…

The workshop is already awake. Metal tools clink against one another. The smell of oil hangs in the air. Five-year-old Youssef sits beside the workbench, close to his father, a mechanic.

For many children, the morning begins with a school uniform and a breakfast before class. For Youssef, the day begins in a place built for adults and their work.

AT HIS FATHER'S SIDE

He is there to help.

His father earns a modest daily income as a mechanic. Youssef accompanies him and takes part in the working day. What may look like a small contribution is part of a much larger household struggle to make ends meet.

But a five-year-old should not have to carry the expectations of an adult workplace. The question is not whether his family needs income. It is why the family has so few alternatives.

AT HOME · ANOTHER SHIFT BEGINS

The pressure reaches beyond Youssef.

His mother works as a housekeeper to help support the family. At home, responsibilities are shared by the children too. His eight-year-old sister helps care for the younger children, while his fourteen-year-old brother also works.

When adults are stretched by poverty and long working days, childhood responsibilities can quietly shift onto children — including the care, time, and attention that younger siblings need.

THE HOUSEHOLD

Every contribution has a reason.

There are mouths to feed, bills to meet, and a newborn in the family. The family's reliance on work is not a simple story of individual choices. It is shaped by limited income, insecure opportunities, and the need to get through each day.

That context matters. A response that simply blames parents or removes a child's income without supporting the household risks making an already difficult situation worse.

THE QUESTION WE MUST ASK

What happens to the future?

Youssef's story is not only about one workshop or one family. It asks what happens when work takes time away from learning, play, rest, and healthy development — and when the next generation inherits the same limited choices.

The answer cannot be to ignore the family's hardship. It must be to protect the child while making it possible for the family to survive without depending on the child's work.

05 / A way forward

Protect children. Support families.

Prohibition is most effective when it is paired with policies that address the pressures pushing children into work.

📚
Quality education

Accessible schooling and targeted support for children at risk of dropping out.

🤝
Social protection

Support vulnerable households so children are not relied on to meet basic needs.

💼
Decent adult work

Better adult employment opportunities and adequate wages.

⚖️
Labour enforcement

Enforce minimum-age rules and protect children from hazardous work.

🏭
Responsible business

Identify and address child labour risks in operations and supply chains.

📣
Community awareness

Help families and employers understand children's rights and available support.

A child's future should never be the price of poverty.

Protect childhood. Invest in education. Break the cycle.
